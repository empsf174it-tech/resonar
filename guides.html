The Complete Guide to Noise Cancellation

Passive isolation vs. ANC, how each architecture works, and how to pick the right level for where you listen and edit.

By Sarah Jenkins, Lead Audio Engineer
Last Updated: Oct 12, 2023

Whether you're editing a podcast in a busy coffee shop or tracking vocals in a home studio, understanding noise cancellation is critical. But the terminology—Passive, Active, Feedforward, Hybrid—can be overwhelming.

Passive Isolation vs. Active Noise Cancellation (ANC)

Passive Isolation is just physical blockage. Thick earpads, closed-back designs, and tight clamping force prevent sound waves from entering your ear canal. It works best for high-frequency sounds like people talking or glass clinking.

Active Noise Cancellation (ANC) uses microphones to listen to the outside world, then plays an inverted sound wave (anti-noise) into your ears to cancel it out. ANC is highly effective against low-frequency, constant drones like airplane engines or AC units.

Types of ANC Architectures

Feedforward

Microphones placed on the outside of the earcup. Reacts quickly, but can't self-correct if the anti-noise isn't perfect.

Feedback

Microphones placed inside the earcup. Hears exactly what you hear and corrects it, but slower to react to sudden noises.

Hybrid

Uses both internal and external microphones. The most effective, but usually found only on premium models.
